\section{Systematic Uncertainties}
\label{sec:sysU}

In this chapter, we discuss the systematic uncertainties considered in this analysis. The primary systematic uncertainty comes from the efficiency correction applied in the unfolding procedure. Additional uncertainties are associated with the requirement on E/p, the 4C kinematic fit, background estimate and the $\piz$ veto.

\subsection{Efficiency correction uncertainties}
\label{sec:eff_sys}

The corrections applied to the MC events in the unfolding procedure are designed to mitigate discrepancies between data and simulation, including the NLO ISR photon production rate and the reconstruction efficiencies for tracking, PID, $\pi^0$ reconstruction, and ISR photon detection. The finite precision of these corrections introduces systematic uncertainties in the unfolded results.

The correction factors are grouped into seven independent sources:
\[
c_{\text{NLO}}(j),\; c_{\text{trk}}(\pi^+),\; c_{\text{pid}}(\pi^+),\; c_{\text{trk}}(\pi^-),\; c_{\text{pid}}(\pi^-),\; c_{\pi^0},\; c_{\gamma_{\text{ISR}}}.
\]
Each factor has an associated uncertainty, derived from auxiliary measurements or theoretical calculations. To propagate these uncertainties to the unfolded spectrum, we perform a toy MC procedure.

For each toy variation $k = 1, \dots, N_{\text{toy}}$, we independently sample all seven correction factors according to their respective uncertainty distributions (Gaussian with widths given by the factor's uncertainty). This yields a set of seven parameters:
\[
\bigl( c_{\text{NLO}}^{(k)}(j),\; c_{\text{trk}}^{(k)}(\pi^+),\; c_{\text{pid}}^{(k)}(\pi^+),\; c_{\text{trk}}^{(k)}(\pi^-),\; c_{\text{pid}}^{(k)}(\pi^-),\; c_{\pi^0}^{(k)},\; c_{\gamma_{\text{ISR}}}^{(k)} \bigr).
\]

For each such parameter set, we recompute the corrected migration matrix $A_{ij}^{\text{corr},(k)}$ following the prescription in Sec.~\ref{sec:corr_signal_kmfit}, then derive the normalized response matrix and perform the unfolding to obtain a toy unfolded spectrum $\{ u_i^{(k)} \}$, where $i$ indexes the truth bins.

After processing all $N_{\text{toy}}$ toys, the covariance matrix of the systematic uncertainty arising from the efficiency corrections is estimated as:
\[
\Sigma_{ij}^{\text{sys}} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{toy}} - 1} \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{toy}}} \left( u_i^{(k)} - u_i^{\text{nom}} \right) \left( u_j^{(k)} - u_j^{\text{nom}} \right),
\]
where $u_i^{\text{nom}}$ is the nominal unfolded spectrum. The diagonal elements $\sqrt{\Sigma_{ii}^{\text{sys}}}$ provide the uncertainty per bin, while the off-diagonal elements quantify correlations between bins induced by the uncertainties in the correction factors. This covariance matrix is taken as the systematic uncertainty contribution from the efficiency corrections in the final results. This procedure is performed independently for each data-taking period (rounds 0304, 15, 16, and 17) and for each $M_{3\pi}$ region. The resulting diagonal systematic uncertainties per bin, expressed as relative uncertainties, are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:syst_efficiency_diag} for all periods and regions.

\subsection{The detector resolution effects in unfolding}

The migration of events between neighboring mass bins due to detector resolution is quantified by the response matrix \(P_{ij}\) constructed from MC simulations. As an example, Fig.~\ref{fig:reco_resolution_example} shows the distribution of reconstructed \(m_{3\pi}\) for events generated in the truth bin \([0.785, 0.790]~\text{GeV}/c^2\) using the round 16 MC sample. A clear broadening is observed around the \(\omega\) meson peak, which reflects the detector resolution as modeled in the MC simulation.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.45\textwidth]{fig/reco_for_truth_bin_0.785-0.790.eps}
    \caption{Distribution of reconstructed $m_{3\pi}$ for MC events generated in the truth bin $[0.785, 0.790]~\text{GeV}/c^2$ (round 16). The spread illustrates the detector resolution effect as modeled in MC, which causes migrations to neighboring bins.}
    \label{fig:reco_resolution_example}
\end{figure}

The observed yield in each mass bin is related to the generator-level yield through the response matrix:
\begin{equation}
    \left( \frac{dN}{dm} \right)_{i}^{\text{obs}} = \sum_{j} P_{ij} \left( \frac{dN}{dm} \right)_{j}^{\text{gen}}
    \label{eq:response_matrix}
\end{equation}
where \(P_{ij}\) is constructed entirely from MC simulations. Both data and MC events suffer from detector resolution broadening, but the response matrix only contains the MC-predicted migration pattern. A key concern is whether the detector resolution in data differs from that in MC, as any mismatch would bias the unfolded cross section.

To quantify this potential discrepancy, we introduce a calibrated response matrix \(P_{ij}^{\text{cal}}(\delta, \kappa)\) that parameterizes the differences between data and MC. For each truth bin \(j\), the mean and standard deviation of the reconstructed mass distribution from MC are defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mu_{0,j} = \frac{\sum_i m_i P_{ij}}{\sum_i P_{ij}}, \qquad 
    \sigma_{0,j} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_i (m_i - \mu_{0,j})^2 P_{ij}}{\sum_i P_{ij}}}
    \label{eq:mc_mean_sigma}
\end{equation}
where \(m_i\) is the center of reconstructed bin \(i\). The data response is then modeled by applying a global shift \(\delta\) and a broadening factor \(\kappa\) to these MC-derived parameters:
\begin{equation}
    \mu_j = \mu_{0,j} + \delta, \qquad \sigma_j = \kappa \sigma_{0,j}
    \label{eq:calibrated_params}
\end{equation}
These calibrated parameters define a Gaussian smearing kernel \(G_{ik}^{(j)}(\delta, \kappa)\) for each truth bin \(j\), which is then convolved with the original MC response matrix to obtain the calibrated transfer matrix:
\begin{equation}
    P_{ij}^{\text{cal}}(\delta, \kappa) = \sum_k G_{ik}^{(j)}(\delta, \kappa) P_{kj}^{(0)}
    \label{eq:calibrated_matrix}
\end{equation}
where \(P_{kj}^{(0)}\) is the nominal MC response matrix. The observed data distribution is then modeled as:
\begin{equation}
    \left( \frac{dN}{dm} \right)_{i}^{\text{obs}} = \sum_j P_{ij}^{\text{cal}}(\delta, \kappa) \left( \frac{dN}{dm} \right)_{j}^{\text{VMD}}
    \label{eq:fit_model}
\end{equation}
By fitting Eq.~\eqref{eq:fit_model} to the observed data using the known VMD lineshape as the true spectrum, the fitted parameters \(\delta\) and \(\kappa\) directly quantify the shift and broadening differences between data and MC in the response matrix. This approach provides a data-driven estimate of the systematic uncertainty arising from detector resolution effects.

\subsection{The requirement on E/p}

To evaluate the systematic uncertainty associated with the $E/p < 0.8$ requirement in the $e^+e^- \to \pi^+\pi^-\pi^0$ process, we adopt a linear approximation as a function of the $3\pi$ invariant mass $M_{3\pi}$. The motivation is that as $M_{3\pi}$ increases, the kinematic range of the individual $\pi^+$ or $\pi^-$ momenta broadens significantly, which in turn affects the response of the $E/p$ selector differently in data and MC. Therefore, the data--MC efficiency ratio of the $E/p$ cut is expected to vary smoothly with $M_{3\pi}$.

We calibrate this dependence using two control samples. The first is the $\omega \to \pi^+\pi^-\pi^0$ region at low $M_{3\pi}$, where we use the selection criteria and samples described in Sec.~\ref{pi0_eff} to select clean $e^+e^- \to \omega \pi^0$ events. The efficiency of the $E/p < 0.8$ requirement is defined as the fraction of events retained after applying the cut. The relative difference between data and MC is quantified as:

\[
\delta_{E/p} = \frac{\varepsilon_{\text{Data}}}{\varepsilon_{\text{MC}}} - 1.
\]

Table~\ref{tab:ep_omega} summarizes the results for the Round~16 dataset in the $\omega$ signal region.

For the high-$M_{3\pi}$ region, we use the control sample of $e^+e^- \to \pi^+\pi^-\pi^0$ near the $J/\psi$ mass, following the analysis in Ref.~\cite{BESIII_MEMO_1606}. From that study, the relative difference between data and MC due to the $E/p$ requirement is:

\[
\delta_{E/p}^{J/\psi} = \frac{0.9}{93.28} = +0.965\%.
\]

Assuming a linear dependence of $\delta_{E/p}$ on $M_{3\pi}$ between the $\omega$ and $J/\psi$ reference points, the systematic uncertainty for an intermediate $M_{3\pi}$ is obtained by linear interpolation:

\[
\delta_{E/p}(M_{3\pi}) = \delta_{E/p}^{\omega} + 
\frac{\delta_{E/p}^{J/\psi} - \delta_{E/p}^{\omega}}{M_{J/\psi} - M_{\omega}}
\cdot (M_{3\pi} - M_{\omega}),
\]

where $M_{\omega} = 0.782$~GeV and $M_{J/\psi} = 3.097$~GeV. For $M_{3\pi}$ below the $\omega$ or above the $J/\psi$, we conservatively take the value of the nearest reference point (constant extrapolation). The resulting data--MC difference in percentage is then taken as the systematic uncertainty for the given $3\pi$ mass bin.

This percentage directly applies to the generated spectrum $\left( \frac{dN}{dm} \right)^{\text{gen}}$, since the efficiency $\varepsilon$ is already incorporated in the unfolding procedure, as first shown in Eq.~\ref{eq:cross_section_relation}:
\[
\left( \frac{dN}{dm} \right)^{\text{gen}} = \sigma_{3\pi}^{\text{dress}}(m) \cdot \frac{dL}{dm} \cdot \varepsilon \cdot \mathcal{B}(\pi^0 \to \gamma\gamma).
\]
Unlike the statistical uncertainty from unfolding, which yields a correlated covariance matrix, this systematic source is assumed to be uncorrelated among mass bins. Therefore, it is implemented as a diagonal covariance matrix and added in quadrature to the total covariance matrix of the cross section measurement.

\subsection{The 4C kinematic fit}
\label{sec:kmfit_sys}

The nominal results for both the measured spectrum $\left( \frac{dN}{dm} \right)$ and the response matrix used in the unfolding procedure are obtained with the $\chi^2_{\text{corr}} < 50$ requirement applied in the 4C kinematic fit. To evaluate the associated systematic uncertainty, we repeat the entire analysis chain — including the estimation of $\left( \frac{dN}{dm} \right)$ and the unfolding procedure — after disabling the correction of the helix parameters for charged tracks in the kinematic fit.

In principle, one could vary the helix parameter corrections according to their uncertainties and repeat the unfolding multiple times to extract the covariance matrix, as described in Sec.~\ref{sec:eff_sys}. However, the uncertainties on these corrections are very small, and such an approach would likely lead to an underestimate of the systematic uncertainty. To be conservative, we instead take half of the difference between the unfolded spectra obtained with the alternative and nominal configurations as the systematic uncertainty for each bin. To account for bin-to-bin correlations, we construct the full covariance matrix as described below.

Let $\Delta_i$ be the difference between the alternative and nominal unfolded spectra in bin $i$:

\begin{equation}
\Delta_i = \left( \frac{dN}{dm} \right)^{\text{alt}}_i - \left( \frac{dN}{dm} \right)^{\text{nom}}_i
\end{equation}

The diagonal elements of the covariance matrix are given by:

\begin{equation}
\text{Cov}_{ii} = \left( \frac{1}{2} \Delta_i \right)^2
\end{equation}

The off-diagonal elements are constructed as:
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{ij} = s_i \cdot s_j \cdot \sqrt{\operatorname{Cov}_{ii} \cdot \operatorname{Cov}_{jj}},
\]
where $s_i = \operatorname{sign}(\Delta_i)$.

This procedure yields a complete covariance matrix that accounts for bin-to-bin correlations: bins with the same sign are assumed to be fully positively correlated ($+1$), while bins with opposite signs are fully anti-correlated ($-1$). The resulting covariance matrix is then added in quadrature to the total covariance matrix of the cross section measurement.

\subsection{Background estimate}

The background in the $\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0$ sample consists mainly of two components. The dominant background arises from the $e^+e^- \to \pi^+\pi^-\pi^0\pi^0$ process. Its contribution is estimated using a data-driven method, where the background level in each $3\pi$ mass bin is evaluated and accompanied by a statistical uncertainty. In the fit to extract the $3\pi$ yield, this background component is constrained via a Gaussian penalty term that incorporates its uncertainty. Therefore, no additional systematic uncertainty is assigned for this dominant background.

For all other remaining background processes, a first- or second-order polynomial is used in the nominal fit, with all parameters left floating. The main systematic uncertainty associated with these backgrounds comes from their shape. To evaluate this effect, we perform an alternative fit in which the background shape is taken from the inclusive MC sample, and the normalization is constrained via a Gaussian term fixed to the expected yield based on the integrated luminosity. The resulting $3\pi$ yield after the alternative fit is then propagated through the full unfolding procedure.

Following the same procedure described in Sec.~\ref{sec:kmfit_sys}, we construct a covariance matrix using $\Delta_i$ (the difference between the alternative and nominal unfolded spectra) instead of $\frac{1}{2}\Delta_i$ for the diagonal elements. The diagonal elements of the covariance matrix are given by:

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{ii} = \Delta_i^2
\]

The off-diagonal elements are constructed as:

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{ij} = s_i \cdot s_j \cdot \sqrt{\operatorname{Cov}_{ii} \cdot \operatorname{Cov}_{jj}},
\]

where $s_i = \operatorname{sign}(\Delta_i)$. This covariance matrix is then added in quadrature to the total covariance matrix of the cross section measurement.


\subsection{The $\pi^0$ veto}

The $\pi^0$ veto condition is applied only in the high-mass region of the spectrum. To evaluate the associated systematic uncertainty, we repeat the analysis — including the unfolding procedure — after removing this veto condition, focusing specifically on the third mass interval where the veto is originally applied.

Following the same procedure described in Sec.~\ref{sec:kmfit_sys}, we construct a covariance matrix using $\Delta_i$ (the difference between the unfolded spectra obtained without and with the $\pi^0$ veto) for the diagonal elements:

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{ii} = \Delta_i^2
\]

The off-diagonal elements are constructed as:

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{ij} = s_i \cdot s_j \cdot \sqrt{\operatorname{Cov}_{ii} \cdot \operatorname{Cov}_{jj}},
\]

where $s_i = \operatorname{sign}(\Delta_i)$. This covariance matrix is then added in quadrature to the total covariance matrix of the cross section measurement.
